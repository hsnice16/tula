# Changelog

Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versions
follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). A version is
chosen when a release is cut, from what went into it — [ROADMAP.md](./ROADMAP.md)
tracks the milestones and states the bump rules.

This is the consumer-facing log: what changes a number tula reports, what it
reads, what it refuses, or how you install it. What does **not** belong here —
CI and build plumbing, refactors, and doc-only edits — stays in commit messages.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Every holding now says how much of it you can actually move, and what is holding the rest.** Ten ETH supplied to Aave against a USDC loan read as ten ETH. That is right about exposure — the price move is still yours — and it reads as holdings, so it corrupts the ordinary decision: *I have ten ETH, I do not need to top up.* Acting on that ETH fails, or liquidates what it was securing. `positions`, and a venue's own `positions`, now carry a **FREE** and an **UNAVAILABLE** column wherever a holding is held, and neither column appears at all where everything is free — an asset nothing is holding does not need two columns saying so. What is unavailable names why, because the reason is the action: collateral securing a debt is released by repaying it, a staked balance by unstaking and waiting out the unbonding period, a balance held against an open order by cancelling the order, and money that has not settled by waiting, which is the one nothing you do at the venue makes sooner. Each of those is spelled out under the table, once per reason actually present. On Aave the free figure is what the protocol will release: the health factor says how much collateral can come out before the market reaches 1.00, and that figure is stated as the lender's own limit rather than as advice, because 1.00 is the level it liquidates at — `breaks` is where what happens if you go there is answered. Where the venue does not report enough to prove a free figure, it is an em dash rather than the whole balance: Kraken states that its holds cover spot non-margin orders only, so beside an open margin position the free number would be too high; and Hyperliquid's per-balance hold is not read at all. Each says which venue could not state it and why. A holding claimed for more than it holds — what a health factor under 1 describes — says so rather than rounding up to nothing, since a position already liquidatable must not read as one that is merely fully pledged. None of this touches a single exposure figure: a pledged asset moves with its price exactly as an unpledged one does, the netted delta is unchanged, and the free quantity never reaches it. The same split, rendered by the same formatters, is in what the model is handed, so an answer in English and the table above it cannot disagree about how much of a holding can move.
- **Every view now says what tula never asked for.** A venue that answers is taken as complete, so half an account read is half an account reported with nothing on screen to say so: somebody who migrated to Aave V4 saw an empty Aave book, no venue failure, no `INCOMPLETE`, and no reason to doubt the total. `/venues` now counts and names the areas of the venues you connected that nothing here reads. It is assembled from what each connector declares about itself, never written out by hand, so it shrinks the day a connector starts reading something and disappears when there is nothing left to say; each declared gap is held open by that connector's own test, so the sentence and the code fail together. What each one declares is the specific thing nothing here calls — Aave V4 and the Safety Module, Kraken Futures and the account margin level, Binance's COIN-M and Portfolio Margin books and its earn products, Coinbase's other portfolios, Hyperliquid's vaults and its borrow/lend book, Stripe Treasury, every chain outside the three read — with the reason and with what the gap costs you. It is a different thing from the lines that do sit beside a figure and says so in its own words: `INCOMPLETE` is a venue — or one of its addresses — that failed, `REMOVED` is a venue this build dropped that you still hold a key for, and this is a venue that answered about part of the account. A run with all of them prints all of them. This line alone raises no exit code, because nothing failed and nothing is missing that a command could go and get; a stored key for a removed venue still exits non-zero, because the book genuinely is short of a venue you connected. A venue that answered and holds nothing gets a sentence of its own: it names the possibility that what you hold is somewhere tula does not read, rather than only offering to replace the address. `venues` names every area, under the venue it belongs to and with what it costs you: whether it may hold value the total is missing, may hide a liquidation, or hides how much of something you can move. A venue's own `status` lists its own. Venues with no connector at all are deliberately not named — that list is unbounded, identical for everyone and actionable by nobody, and a line read as wallpaper takes the parts that *are* about your account down with it — and neither is a venue you chose not to connect, which is a decision rather than a gap. `get_venue_status` hands the model the same list, so a question about completeness is answered in full.
- **A venue can watch more than one account, and every figure counts all of them.** A venue held one credential and connecting it again wrote over what was there, so a second wallet — or a second exchange key — could not be watched at all. Connecting a venue that already holds one now asks first: add another, or replace one of these. Adding keeps what is there and reads both, so a book spread over a hot wallet and a cold one is one book, and every row carries the account it came from — named by what you called it, or by the address itself — so `breaks` says which wallet to go and act on rather than only which venue. Naming one is offered from the second onward and never asked for before that: a name is only worth typing when there are two to tell apart, and it is optional even then. Replacing goes through the same gate every other deletion here goes through — the entry named on screen, the venue's own name typed out, and never Enter, which is the key that got you to the question — and it is asked only after the new credential has been checked, so a key that turns out to be wrong never costs you the one on disk. The same address in two checksum spellings is one address and the second is refused, because two entries for one account double every holding in it. One account failing costs its own rows and no others: what the rest returned stays in the book, `INCOMPLETE` names the account that went rather than only the venue, and the exit is still non-zero. A venue watching five addresses is one venue in `/`, in ctrl+k and in the venue table; the addresses are not venues and are never offered as any. `/<venue> disconnect <name or address>` forgets one of them and drops only its positions, `/<venue> disconnect` on its own over a venue holding several says which ones there are rather than guessing, and `/forget <venue>` is still the spelling that means all of them — it now names each one it is about to take. Connecting unattended from the command line adds rather than replaces and says so: a run nobody is watching may not delete a credential, and there is no terminal there to name one on. None of this puts a new kind of field on the connect screen — at one address or at ten, the only masked box is one the venue's own key earned, and tula still never asks for a seed phrase.
- **One address, three chains: Ethereum, Arbitrum One and Base.** A wallet read now covers native ETH and the ERC-20s a Token Lists feed names for each of the three, and Aave v3 is read on all three — Ethereum's four markets, plus the Arbitrum One and Base deployments, whose Pool addresses are the ones in the register Aave's own documentation defers to and were each confirmed to carry code on that chain and nowhere else. Nothing is entered twice: a public address is the whole credential, and it is the same address on every chain. A holding nets across the chains it sits on, so USDC in three places is one USDC figure rather than three rows that never cancel — and every row still carries the chain it came from, labelled `wallet-arbitrum` and `aave-base` beside `wallet` and `aave` the way the Aave markets already were, because a book that cannot say where a position is cannot say what to act on. Each chain carries its own freshness, so a node minutes behind ages its own rows and not the book above them.
- **A node that is not the chain it was configured as is refused rather than believed.** This is the failure with nothing wrong-looking about it: token lists are filtered per chain, and a call to an address holding no contract answers `0x` rather than an error — so an Arbitrum token list read against an Ethereum node comes back as a zero for every token, and a chain somebody has money on is reported as an empty wallet with nothing beside it to say the question was never really asked. Each node is now asked which chain it is before any answer of its is used, and one pointed somewhere else fails that chain by name.
- **A failure says which chain it happened on, and which variable to change.** Every RPC error opened "the Ethereum node" and ended by naming `TULA_ETH_RPC`; on a book reading three nodes that is a sentence sending the reader to replace an endpoint that is answering. Each chain has a public default node and an override of its own — `TULA_ARBITRUM_RPC` and `TULA_BASE_RPC` beside `TULA_ETHEREUM_RPC` — and each one moves only its own chain. `TULA_ETH_RPC` still points Ethereum: it is the name the one-chain release shipped and it is in dotfiles already, so it is an alias rather than a variable quietly ignored, and where both are set the new name wins. `TULA_TOKEN_LIST` is unchanged and still applies to every chain, with a per-chain name where one chain wants a different feed. A chain that fails now costs its own rows and no others: what the other two returned stays in the book, and each chain that did not answer is a line of its own naming it — where before, one node rate-limiting took the whole venue off the screen. Every chain failing at once is still `INCOMPLETE` and a non-zero exit, never an empty book.
- **A rate-limited node costs a retry rather than the chain.** A public node answering `429` took its whole chain off the book: Aave came back `INCOMPLETE` with a line naming a node nobody chose and a variable most readers will not set, and the only way through was to run `/refresh` until the node was in a better mood. Each chain now ships three public nodes and moves to the next when the one it is on rate-limits it, times out or goes down — including on the call that asks a node which chain it is, which is the one that ran first and so the one that used to fail. A chain stays on the node that answered for the rest of the run rather than starting over each time, so a busy node is not asked again on every batch and one book is never read half from one node and half from another at two different heights. The cost is stated where the rest of the egress is: on a run where the first node answers it is the only one contacted, and a chain that has moved has shown the address to a second operator. Every node it may reach is listed by name in [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md), and each was checked against the chain itself: that it answers for the chain it is filed under, that it takes a batch the size tula sends, and that it stays answering under the load of a real wallet read rather than rate-limiting partway through. Setting `TULA_ETHEREUM_RPC`, `TULA_ARBITRUM_RPC` or `TULA_BASE_RPC` replaces that chain's list outright — several may be given, comma-separated — and nothing public is ever appended to it: which nodes see the address is a decision, not a fallback. Rotation is only for a node that will not talk. A node that *answers* and contradicts itself — a batch answered twice, an id nobody asked for, a chain id that is not the chain — still fails loudly rather than being asked again somewhere quieter, and a node the network is intercepting is reported rather than rotated past. Every node reached mid-rotation is asked which chain it is before a single row is read off it.
- **A bridged stablecoin is its own row, and never nets into the thing it is named after.** `USDC.e` on Arbitrum and `USDbC` on Base are issued by a bridge rather than by Circle and are not redeemable at Circle, and `USDT0` is an omnichain issue rather than the Ethereum USDT. Netted together, a bridge losing its peg would be averaged away against the asset it had failed to be, and the row actually at risk would never appear. Each is carried as itself. That a price source quotes one of them at a dollar is that source's claim about a peg, and it arrives attached to the number rather than built into the asset the number is filed under. Solana and Hyperliquid's own EVM chain are read by nothing, and both connectors say so rather than leaving it to be noticed: a HyperEVM balance is two linked balances — a HyperCore spot balance and an EVM token scaled against it — and stating either alone would be a number that is not the holding.

### Changed

- **A ranking of what breaks first says what it could not see.** One caveat survived leaving every view, and only on the two commands that rank a liquidation. `breaks` and `shock` claim more than the views that state a quantity: they say what can be called in, in order. A venue in your book with an unread area that could hold a liquidation of its own makes that order *wrong* rather than short, so those two now carry one line naming those venues, and `/venues` names the areas. It is narrowed twice — to areas that hide a liquidation, and to venues that actually returned rows — so it shortens as those close, disappears with the last of them, and never names a venue this book is not held at. `<venue> breaks` says it about that venue alone. The model is handed the same fact on the same two results and on no others.
- **A book that comes back empty names the third possibility.** Connect Aave, hold everything on V4, and every view said "either the account is empty, or it is not the one you trade with" — two explanations, both wrong, and the reader goes to re-check an address that was right the whole time. The screen a venue's own view draws has named the third possibility since the disclosure shipped; the one drawn when the *whole book* is empty never did, which is the case where it is most likely to be the answer. It does now, and points at the venue status screen that lists what is unread.
- **What tula does not read is answered where you ask, and no longer printed beside every figure.** A `NOT READ` line sat under every view, counting the areas of your connected venues that nothing here calls — sixteen of them on a book held at three venues, and the same sixteen tomorrow. That count cannot reach zero: a read-only tool has unbounded uncovered surface by construction, so the line was a permanent apology in the block reserved for things you can act on today, and a block read as wallpaper takes `INCOMPLETE` down with it — which is the line about a venue that failed *this* read. The rule is now the one `src/core/coverage.ts` already applied to venues with no connector at all, one level up: a gap that closes gets a line, a gap that does not gets a command. Nothing was hidden and nothing was dropped. `/venues` names every area, grouped by venue, with what each may hide; a venue's own `status` names its own; and the third possibility under a venue that answered holding nothing — that what you hold is in a part of it nothing here reads — is unchanged, because that is the case where a gap and an empty account look identical. The model is held to the same line: no tool result volunteers a coverage caveat except the two that rank a liquidation, which is its own entry below; `get_venue_status` carries the whole list, and its description tells the model to call it whenever an answer turns on the data being complete — so a question about completeness is answered in full and a question about your ETH is answered with your ETH. The two surfaces are short of the same things by construction, which `src/consistency.test.ts` pins in both directions.
- **A fixture nobody could capture, and the reason nobody could.** Hyperliquid's isolated-position margin was declared unread with the blocker stated as "no captured account held an isolated position to measure the split against". The reason turned out to be the address source: `scripts/capture-onchain.ts` samples the followers of the HLP vault, and **not one of the 99 addresses that call returns holds an isolated position** — they are passive depositors. Thirteen of the top two hundred of the public mainnet leaderboard do, and five hold isolated and cross at once. The script now takes `--account <address>` to capture one by name, classifies that shape, and the fixture is in the tree with the arithmetic pinned: the cross summary excludes an isolated leg's margin and the whole-account summary includes it, so reading the second as the cross pool overstates it by a whole position. Two defects in the script fell out of looking: the follower list carries the literal string `Leader` among the addresses, which would have ended a whole capture run with an HTTP error the first time the loop reached it, and an address check was written against a `/g` regex, which answers alternately true and false for the same input. One sharper finding is recorded rather than fixed: the relation this repo checks every liquidation price against is a *cross* relation, and run over an isolated leg it reports the venue's own stated price as wrong by 22%. Nothing computes that figure today — the connector quotes what the venue states — and the task file now says it must stay that way until the isolated case is derived from the margin posted to that leg alone.
- **Every declared gap now has a task file, and the build fails if one does not.** Thirty areas are declared unread across seven venues, and thirteen of them had nowhere to go — two, Coinbase's CFTC-regulated futures and Kraken's drawn credit lines, were mentioned in no file in the repository at all, and both hide money. A fourteenth, Kraken's account margin level, was filed under the portfolio aggregator, which is the one place `ROADMAP.md`'s own rule says a liquidation-hiding gap may never go. Each gap now names the task that would close it in the connector manifest itself, beside the declaration, so the two are one edit; `src/coverage-plan.test.ts` fails the build on a plan that is not a real task, on one filed under a task already finished, on one hiding a liquidation filed under the aggregator, and on any plan `ROADMAP.md`'s table does not name. Writing it turned up a thirty-first area that was declared nowhere, and it is fixed below rather than filed. [ROADMAP.md](./ROADMAP.md) now carries a table pointing each one at the work that closes it, and nine new task files hold the detail: Aave's Safety Module, isolation mode and stable-rate debt; Hyperliquid's spot hold, isolated margin, staking, vaults, sub-accounts, borrow/lend book and builder-deployed dexes; the wallet's staking tokens, LP receipts and NFTs; and the chains outside the three, HyperEVM and Solana among them. Two entries moved out of the deferred sections in the process. HyperEVM was filed as refused while the refusal was really about reading half a linked balance — reading both halves is ordinary work, and the refusal now says so in the words that survive its own test. Solana was filed as unscheduled and now has a milestone. 


- **A venue this build dropped is explained once, then named in one line.** The whole account of it — why the venue went, that `/forget` takes tula's copy and revoking the key at the venue is a separate act — was printed under every view for as long as the shell was open, which is two hundred characters of the same paragraph under every table somebody drew. The fact has to persist, because a credential for a venue tula no longer reads is a secret sitting on disk that its owner has forgotten about; the explanation does not. The first view of a session carries the whole of it and every view after it says `REMOVED — circle, still stored.` with the command that removes it. On the command line each `tula` is a fresh process with no session to carry anything through, so a one-shot command prints the whole thing every time — that reader is exactly the one who has not been told. Nothing changes for somebody who never connected the venue: they still see nothing at all, and the exit code still says the book is short of a venue somebody connected.
- **`/update` says when it could not check, instead of saying you are current.** Every failure — no network, an unreachable GitHub, a release page with no version number on it — became the same sentence as a successful check that found nothing newer — the running version named as the newest release — and exit 0. The daily check stays silent on failure, because nobody opened tula to find out about tula; a `/update` somebody typed is the opposite case. It now says whether the running build is current is unknown, names the releases page to check by hand, and exits non-zero.
- **Three dead ends now name the way out.** A credentials file written by a newer tula said "Update tula" where its neighbours hand over a command to run; an update archive with no binary in it was the one failure on that path that did not say nothing had been installed; and the connect screen rendered a failure that is not a venue's refusal — an `ENOSPC` out of writing the key, say — as one bare line, on the surface where a user has least idea what to do next. That screen reads the same failure renderer the shell does now.
- **`credentials.json` holds a list per venue now, is migrated where it stands, and nothing has to be reconnected.** `put` adds rather than replaces, the same credential twice is refused instead of doubling every position it holds, one address in two checksum spellings is one address, replacing one entry swaps it in the place it already held and leaves its siblings untouched, and removing one leaves the rest. A name is the handle on an entry, and it is stored as one line and refused rather than truncated where it would not fit beside a figure. The migration runs through the write-beside-and-rename every other write uses, so an interrupted one leaves the file that was already there rather than half of a new one, and migrating twice does not store anything twice. The file also records the shape it was written in, which earlier versions did not, so a tula meeting a file written by a newer one refuses it and says to update rather than reading only the parts it recognises. The other direction is what the shape on disk is chosen for: a pre-stamp tula still finds the venue under its own key and is handed a list where a credential goes, so it fails that venue out loud instead of reporting it unconnected and offering to take a key it is already holding. That shape is pinned by a test; that the shipped v0.1.3 binary then behaves as described was checked by hand, and nothing here spawns an old binary to check it again.

### Removed

- **Circle Mint is gone, and a Circle key you connected is no longer read.** Circle Mint issues one kind of API key and it can create payouts and transfers; restricted keys are a Circle Console feature over Wallets, Contracts, Webhooks and Swap Kit, and Circle Mint is not among them. tula's rule is that a credential able to move funds is refused at connect, so the venue cannot be offered at all — reporting the permission as `unknown` was honest only while nobody had checked, and it has now been checked. If you had Circle connected, the key is still in `~/.config/tula/credentials.json` and tula will not touch it for you: every command that reads your book now names Circle as removed — with the whole account of it the first time you ask, and the fact and the way out on every view after that — `/forget circle` deletes tula's copy, and the key itself has to be revoked in the Circle Mint console.

### Fixed

- **An Aave account in eMode was read against the wrong liquidation thresholds, and some of its collateral was not read as collateral at all.** Aave liquidates an account in an eMode category against that *category's* threshold, not each reserve's own — 95% against WETH's 83% on Ethereum, and on one measured account the LTV Aave states is only reproducible with the category's figure, where the reserve parameters alone are out by 3,100 basis points. Every shock this build weights, and every collateral share `breaks` computes, was measured against a bar the lender would not use, sitting under a health factor Aave itself stated, which is why nothing about the row looked wrong. Worse than the weighting: whether a leg counted as collateral at all was gated on the reserve's own threshold, and Aave has live reserves at a reserve threshold of zero that are full collateral inside a category at 92-95% — six on Ethereum Core alone. Those legs were not weighted wrongly, they stopped being collateral legs: no liquidation figure on them, and missing from the list the debt beside them says it is covered by. Both are now read from the category the account is actually in, and a collateral asset *outside* that category keeps its own threshold, because Aave still counts it rather than excluding it. Verified against live accounts on Ethereum, Arbitrum One and Base. The obvious way to do this is wrong and was the plan until it was checked against the chain: the reserve configuration still carries an eMode field, and it still reads correctly for assets listed before Aave's Liquid eMode upgrade — WETH and wstETH among them, which are the ones anybody would spot-check — while every asset listed since reads zero whatever category it is in. Membership is read from the category's own bitmap instead. None of this was in a released build: the multi-chain Aave read it belongs to is new here.


- **A refresh in which a venue fails no longer takes that venue's holdings off the book.** `refresh` is the command somebody runs to recover from a failure, and it was the one command that could make the book smaller: it rebuilt from nothing and replaced the cache at the end, so a venue that failed left with its rows. A total read a minute earlier fell by that venue's whole value, under an `INCOMPLETE` block saying a venue was missing and not that its holdings had just left the sum — and where every venue failed, the reader was left with no book at all rather than the one they had. A venue that fails and returns nothing now keeps the rows it last returned. Nothing is presented as current: every row carries the time it was read, so it dates itself in the `AS OF` column of every table it appears in, `/venues` counts and dates it beside the failure, and the note under the table says in words that its rows are the last read rather than this one. A venue that answered in *part* — one chain of three, one address of two — keeps nothing, because it has fresh rows on the book already and a previous row beside them would be the same holding twice with one of the two wrong.
- **A failed venue's rows were counted in a total the model was told they were missing from.** A venue that fails and returns nothing keeps the rows it last returned, so those figures are in every sum on screen. The flag handed to the model said flatly that anything listed there is left out of the totals — so an answer in plain English could tell somebody their exposure excluded a venue whose whole position was inside the number they were reading, which is the one kind of wrong figure this tool exists to prevent. It now says a listed venue's figures are missing *or* are its previous read, and names `get_venue_status` as the thing that says which. No table changes; what changes is the sentence written over one.
- **A shocked health factor now says when it moved only one side of the ratio.** The figure reprices the collateral and holds the debt at today's value, which is true of a stablecoin borrow and wrong of a market that borrowed the asset being shocked — and it was said in a comment in the source rather than to the person acting on the number. `shock` and `run_scenario` now check the assumption against the book: where the scenario moves an asset that market has borrowed, both name the market, the borrowed asset, and which side of the shown figure the true one falls on — a debt worth less is a debt more easily covered, so a fall in what was borrowed leaves the real factor higher, and a rise leaves it lower, which is the dangerous way round. Where the shock touches nothing the market borrowed, which is the common case, neither says anything: a caveat under every figure is a caveat nobody reads.
- **A price source that answers and prices nothing now names the way back.** A source can return 200 and match none of the symbols you hold — nothing fails, nothing throws, and every total quietly has no number behind it. The remedy on that screen was reached only from a quote that threw, so switching to such a source reported `Pricing from X. 0 assets priced.` and stopped there: a dead end with no way out of it. It now says the source answered and priced none of the assets you hold, that the quantities are still correct, and which source to go back to. A book with nothing in it is held apart from this, because it is the same count and not a source that failed.
- **A shocked health factor was weighted by value alone, so every Aave scenario was slightly wrong and some were wrong about liquidation.** Aave secures a debt with each reserve's *thresholded* value, not its market value, and the connector had been decoding that threshold onto every collateral leg — where nothing read it. `shock` and `run_scenario` weighted the market's collateral base by price times quantity, which treats a reserve pledged at 83% as though it were pledged at the same rate as one at 78%. On a market holding 10 ETH at 0.83 beside 0.5 WBTC at 0.78, `shock ETH -30` said the factor lands at 1.18 when it lands at 1.17, and `shock ETH -51` said *nothing liquidates at this level* about a market that is called at that level — both legs are now named. The threshold is read off the leg because one symbol carries different ones in different Aave markets, and the weighting is per market for the same reason. Where a venue publishes no threshold for one leg, that whole market falls back to value alone rather than counting the silent leg as pledged in full: read as 100% it would outweigh every real reserve, which is a worse number than the approximation it replaces.
- **A shock with an odd argument left one asset out of the scenario without saying so.** `shock ETH -20 BTC` parsed the pair it could and dropped `BTC`, and the heading listed only what parsed — so the answer on screen was about a different question than the one typed, with nothing to say BTC had been ignored. The trailing word is now named and the command refuses rather than answering.
- **A Stripe balance no longer renders `FREE —` under a legend saying the figure cannot be proven.** Stripe states what is available outright. The connector had declared its deliberate skip of `instant_available` — a slice of `available`, skipped so the same money is not counted twice — as an *availability* gap, which is the declaration that makes a venue's free figure unprovable, so every Stripe spot row hid a figure Stripe had published.
- **A bad `TULA_PRICE_PAGES` no longer unprices the whole book in silence.** `TULA_PRICE_PAGES=` gave 0 and a typo gave `NaN`; either left the page loop with nothing to run, cached the empty result and threw nothing — so no price error was reported, nothing said so, and the book said "no price for any of N asset(s)" about a list it never fetched. A value that is not a whole page count is now refused by name, and clearing the variable means the default rather than zero pages.
- **The USDC behind a Hyperliquid perp was counted twice, and on a long it was counted as a debt.** `totalRawUsd` is not a balance. Hyperliquid states `accountValue = totalRawUsd + Σ signed notional`, so it is the cash leg of a spot-equivalent view of the perp book — long the coin, short the dollars — and tula published it as a spendable USDC holding beside the account's real spot USDC. On one live account that read as eleven times the account's whole value in USDC held. Behind a leveraged long the same field is negative, and the row said the account owed tens of thousands of USDC it does not owe. The leg stays, because it is the account's own equity and the portfolio total is wrong without it; what changes is that it is now margin — labelled `collateral`, with every cross perp naming it as what it dies with, and an isolated position naming nothing, since an isolated position dies alone. The venue's identity is now asserted against captured responses from six real accounts on every test run — the figures scaled, the arithmetic theirs — which is what would have caught this: the fixture it replaces left `totalNtlPos` out, so there was no arithmetic in it that could disagree.
- **A Kraken margin book reported no exposure and nothing to liquidate.** Kraken's own documentation says opening a margin position produces no debit or credit to your balances beyond the fee, and balances were the only thing tula read — so a 5x long sat in `/0/private/OpenPositions` while the venue answered, successfully, that the account held nothing and nothing could be called. Open positions are read now, as the two things a margin position actually is: the asset it is long or short, and the loan that paid for it, each naming the other. `verifyScope` refuses a key that cannot reach them and says which permission to add — *Query open orders & trades*, which is a query permission and does not allow trading, so nothing here asks for a broader key than tula would hold. What Kraken does not publish is a liquidation price: it liquidates the account on a margin level rather than the position on a price, so these rows reach `breaks` ranked `unknown` with their leverage beside them, which is what they are.
- **Coinbase showed the cash behind a perpetual and never the position.** The accounts list returns a perpetual-futures account, so a book one move from liquidation read as a pile of USDC. Perpetual positions are read from the portfolio the key is scoped to now, with the liquidation price and leverage Coinbase publishes; a position it gave no price for is ranked `unknown` rather than dropped.
- **An Aave market you supply to without pledging is no longer an empty book.** Aave counts a reserve toward your collateral total only where you have switched that reserve on as collateral, so supplying for yield, holding an asset listed at a zero threshold, or simply turning collateral off makes a whole market report zeros — and tula read those zeros as "nothing here" and never asked what was in it. Every market's balances are read now, whatever the totals say. The other half of the same defect: every aToken with a balance was labelled collateral, handed the market's health factor and listed as what a debt is covered by, including reserves that secure nothing and cannot be seized. Those are ordinary holdings now, and a debt names only the collateral actually standing behind it.
- **A balance the node did not answer for is not a balance of zero.** A wallet read dropped any ERC-20 whose call failed, and an absent row is indistinguishable from not holding the token — the quiet wrong answer, on a venue where the node is a public one that rate-limits. It fails the venue and prints `INCOMPLETE` now, as the Aave read already did. Two more of the same shape underneath: a batch answered with a single rejection left forty calls silently unanswered rather than reporting the refusal, and a native ETH balance that came back with neither a result nor an error was read as an empty wallet. A node that answers one call twice, or answers a call tula never made, now fails instead of filing one token's balance under another's row.
- **A second Kraken wallet was a wallet nobody read.** `/0/private/Balance` answers for one wallet and takes an `account_id` to name another; tula passed none, so anything held outside the default wallet was missing from a portfolio that reported itself complete. Every wallet Kraken lists is read now, each under its own label — `kraken-spot` beside `kraken-main` — the way the Aave markets already are. Listing them needs no permission at all, so an existing key reaches it.
- **Stripe reported two of its six balance buckets.** Money for Issued Cards, money reserved against connected accounts, and refund and dispute prefunding are all the account's, none of them is in `available` or `pending`, and none of them was in the book. Each is read now under its own label, because a single Stripe row would say that a balance no payout can reach is money in hand. `instant_available` is deliberately still not read: it is the part of the available balance payable out instantly, so a row for it would state the same money twice.
- **Binance margin positions are read, where Binance will let a reading key read them.** Cross and isolated margin carry a liquidation price and a margin level that appear in no other endpoint, and neither was asked for. Whether reading them needs the borrow permission is undocumented in either direction — so the call is made, and a permission refusal is taken as an account with no margin on it rather than as a failure, the same way the futures leg already behaves. Anything else still fails the venue.
- **Fiat under a withdrawal hold was shown as money you could spend.** Kraken carries it as its own asset — `USD.HOLD` beside `ZUSD` — and the suffix was matched a letter at a time, so `.HOLD` fell through as ordinary spot and was added to the freely spendable row. It is a row of its own now. The same split runs on what Kraken, Binance and Coinbase each report as held or locked: it is your money and it is exposure, so it stays in the book, but it is not what you can move and no longer says it is. Kraken's holds cover spot non-margin orders only, so beside an open margin position the free figure would be too high, so tula withholds it on every unclaimed Kraken row rather than guessing which rows are safe to state.
- **Nine live Kraken assets were renamed to something else.** The connector stripped the first character off any four-character code beginning with X or Z, on the theory that those are the legacy ISO-style prefixes. Against Kraken's public asset list that renamed Tether Gold to AUT, and ZORA, ZETA, ZEUS, XION, XNAP, XTER, ZAMA and ZBCN likewise — every one of them enabled and tradeable, and each landing either unpriceable or under somebody else's ticker. Kraken publishes the answer in each asset's `altname` and tula now reads it, with the prefix rule left only for a code the list did not carry. The test is run against a captured copy of that list rather than an invented one, and it checks all 841 enabled assets for two that would collide.
- **A Binance quarterly netted against nothing.** A delivery contract is named `BTCUSDT_250926`, and the quote currency was stripped from the end of the symbol, so the settlement date stayed part of the asset: one BTC book held across a perp and two quarterlies read as three separate assets, none of them BTC. The date is cut first now, and `BTCUSD_PERP` resolves the same way.
- **Two different tokens that share a ticker are two rows.** The live Uniswap token list carries two mainnet tokens called LIT — Litentry and Lighter — and tula keyed a wallet holding by its symbol, so the two balances were added together and priced once as whichever the price source meant. A contested symbol now carries the head of its contract address, and neither is given a price rather than one of them being given the wrong one. WETH held in a wallet also nets as ETH now, as it always has when Aave reported it; the two spellings were one asset shown as two rows that never cancelled.
- **A health factor moves by the share of the collateral that was shocked, not by all of it.** Every Aave collateral leg carries its market's one health factor, and `shock` treated whichever asset it was given as though it were the whole of what secures the debt. On 10 WETH beside 1 WBTC at 1.42, `shock WETH -30` reported 1.42 -> 0.99 and named the position LIQUIDATED; WETH is 40% of that collateral, so the market ends at 1.25 and nothing is called. The move is now weighted by each leg's share of the base, and a market holding a leg nobody could price reports no new factor at all rather than a computed one.
- **A position already past its liquidation price is gone under any shock, and `shock` now says so.** `breaks` and `shock` gave opposite answers about the same row. A long perp whose mark has already fallen through its trigger reports its distance as a *rise* — that is what it would take to get back — so `breaks` read the flag beside it and said "liquidatable now" while `shock ETH -30` read only the sign of the number and answered that nothing liquidates. The reassuring answer was the wrong one, on the one row a reader must not miss. A shock in either direction now lists it, because there is no move under which it survives.
- **A leveraged position the venue said nothing about is a row, not an absence.** `breaks` dropped every perp with no liquidation price instead of ranking it last, and both perp venues reach that state on purpose — Hyperliquid sends null and Binance sends 0 on a position it will not yet call. A book of nothing but those answered "Nothing here can be liquidated — no leverage, no borrowing, nothing to call", which is a leveraged account being told it has nothing to lose. Those rows now sit at the bottom of the table reading `unknown`, which is what they are: not rankable, and not safe.
- **A percentage that is not a scenario is refused instead of answered.** `shock ETH -150` repriced a book to less than nothing and reported the negative total, and `-100` reported `$0.00` about a real account with no note saying a price had gone missing. A move that leaves an asset priced at nothing or less now leaves it unpriced and named as such, and a figure too large to be a question — `1e400` reaches the engine as a number rather than an error — reprices nothing.
- **How old a price is now travels with it.** Freshness was read off the positions alone, so a CoinPaprika quote hours old was published under an `AS OF` of two seconds; a CoinGecko price held in cache for its full minute was stamped as though it had just arrived. A notional is now dated by the older of the balance and the price behind it, and a quote timestamp that does not parse falls back to when the list was received rather than reaching the screen.
- **Switching price source cannot unprice an asset over how a venue spells it.** Two of the four sources answered under the upper-cased ticker and two under the spelling they were asked for, so an asset a venue names in lower case lost its price on two of them and kept it on the other two. All four answer under the spelling asked for now. For the same reason `purr` from a wallet and `PURR` from Hyperliquid are one netted row rather than two that never cancel.
- **A sub-cent price keeps its digits.** Prices were rendered by the same code as totals, which fixes two decimal places because a total is read to the cent. Every k-prefixed Hyperliquid perp — kPEPE, kSHIB, kBONK — trades below one, so a liquidation price showed as `$0.00`: a trigger reported as unreachable. The same trigger was rendered as money on its way to the model too, so the question asked in English came back with that `$0.00` beside a distance on the same row that was right. Prices now keep four significant digits below a dollar wherever they are drawn, which is also what shows a stablecoin trading at 0.9987 rather than at par.
- **A percentage always carries its direction, and a balance that rounds away is not negative.** A liquidation buffer clamped to zero printed `0.0%` — the one figure on screen with no sign on it — because the zero it was clamped to kept the sign of the subtraction that reached it. A holding below the eight-decimal floor printed `-0`. A timestamp a venue sent that does not parse printed `Invalid  (NaNd ago)`, which reads as a fault in tula rather than as the one thing it means: we do not know how old this is.
- **A venue nobody could reach is no longer described as an account that may be empty.** `/<venue> positions` branched on how many rows came back and not on whether the venue had answered, so a rate-limited node produced "Either the account is empty, or it is not the one you meant to connect" and an offer to replace the address — which is the wrong thing to do about a node that did not answer. `/<venue> breaks` had the same shape and one worse consequence: "Nothing at aave can be liquidated" about a lending venue nobody read. Both now separate the three states the reader has to tell apart — never read, read and empty, read and holding nothing that can be called in — and say which one they are looking at.
- **A venue tula removed is not a venue that failed.** A stored Circle Mint credential made every command print `INCOMPLETE — 1 venue(s) failed`, and Circle did not fail: it is not in the build, so nothing was asked of it. Removed venues now have their own block, which says so and carries the reason, and the failure count is the count of venues that were actually asked and did not answer. `/venues` had been saying `REMOVED — see below` in its own table for exactly this reason.
- **A venue that is connected and holding nothing exists when you ask about it in English.** The agent's venue list was built out of positions, so a venue that returned no rows produced no entry at all — and an empty list is what the tool reads as nothing being connected, so the model was told to say no venue is connected while `/venues` listed it as `connected, holding nothing`. Every venue that was asked is now in that list, holding nothing included.
- **The model is told the venue you connected, not a label you never chose.** One Aave address across its six markets returns rows spelled `aave-prime` and `aave-base`; every screen folds those back to `aave`, and the agent's own tools did not — so a question about where an asset sits named a venue that is not in the menu, one turn after being told the only venue connected was `aave`. The market is still there as its own field for anything that needs it.
- **Asking what a drop would do no longer answers "nothing" about positions nothing could evaluate.** `shock` on the command line has always shown health factors and named what liquidates; the same question asked in English returned neither, and a position whose venue published no liquidation data appeared in no field of the answer at all — not among the liquidated, not among the unpriced — under a note saying the figures were final. So a perp the tool could not rank was narrated as one that survives. It now comes back named, with the market's health factor before and after beside it, and the health factor is the market's rather than one leg's.
- **A question the model declines no longer breaks every question after it.** A refusal arriving *after* the model had called a tool dropped the tool results and left the request for them standing, and the API rejects a tool call with nothing answering it — so from that moment the session answered nothing at all, and the only way back was quitting the shell. The whole exchange is rolled back to before the question now, which is what the failure path beside it already did.
- **A venue whose error quotes a URL still gets told what to do next.** Every failed venue is followed by the command that explains it, unless the failure already names one — and the test for that was whether the text contained a slash. A venue's own error carries one whenever it quotes a link, so a 503 served through a CDN, which names `/cdn-cgi/...` in its body, reported a problem with no way out of it. The test is now whether the line names a command tula actually has.
- **`tula refresh` and `tula exposure` no longer disagree about the same state.** A price source that did not answer made every risk command exit non-zero and left `/refresh` and `/venues` exiting zero, so `tula refresh && tula exposure` reported success and then failure over one book. All of them now answer the same question — is anything missing from this view — and `/venues` says the price source did not answer rather than leaving the exit code to carry it alone.
- **A mistyped command says which word was wrong.** `tula exposre` printed the whole usage block and exited 1, with nothing naming the typo and no suggestion, while the same typo in the shell has answered "Did you mean /exposure?" since the first release. It now names the word and the nearest command. Remedies printed by a one-shot run are also spelled the way they would be typed there: `tula connect kraken`, not `/kraken connect`, which pasted into a real shell is a path that does not exist.
- **tula's own warning is no longer cut off mid-word.** The 200-character bound exists for text a venue wrote; it was being applied to tula's own messages too, and the longest of those is the one that says a request was redirected and nothing was sent on — the highest-severity thing tula says. It lost its last line, which is the one that says what to make of it. A venue's text is still bounded exactly as before. On the same line: four connectors open their error with their own name, so a failure read `binance: Binance: HTTP 451`; the venue is named once now.
- **ctrl+c answers on the two screens that fill the terminal.** The credentials screen is the first thing a new user sees, and ctrl+c did nothing there — Ink holds raw mode, so nothing raised SIGINT either, and the tool read as hung at its own opening screen. It leaves tula now, from that screen and from the connect flow, including while a key is being checked against the venue, which is the wait during which somebody most wants it. Esc leaves the credentials screen on a first run too, which is what "Continue without one" already offered.
- **A keystroke that shares a chunk with a mouse report is not dropped.** Pointer tracking reports every movement while a list is up, so a hand resting on the trackpad can put a report in front of the next character in the same read. The report was handled and the character was discarded: `/shock ETH -20` went in as `/shock ETH -2` — a different scenario, with nothing on screen to say a character had gone.
- **A column is padded in the cells a terminal draws, not the code units a string measures.** An asset symbol is spelled by the venue, and one CJK character measures one and is drawn two — so the ASSET column came up short on exactly that row, every column right of it moved, and the row wrapped, which is the row Ink counts as one and never takes back. Tables and the transcript's own row counting both measure in cells now, so `… N more lines` counts the rows a block really occupies, and a wrap never cuts a surrogate pair or a joined emoji in half.
- **A holding worth less than a cent no longer reads as worth nothing.** A priced, non-zero balance rounded to `$0.00` in the NOTIONAL column, and `$0.00` beside a real holding is indistinguishable from worthless — the same confusion an em dash exists to prevent, arrived at from the other side: `usd(null)` has always printed `—` so that an unknown price could never be mistaken for a zero value, while a known one under half a cent printed the zero itself. On one live Aave book three rows read `$0.00` at once. A sub-cent total now reads `<$0.01`, and a sub-cent debt `-<$0.01`. The cents stay fixed at two places rather than gaining significant digits the way a price did, because a total is read to the cent and a column of them is scanned down the decimal point — so the one figure that cannot be shown there says so rather than being spelled out at a width nothing else in the column is. The same defect one column left: a quantity below `0.00000001` rendered `0`, so a real WSTETH dust balance drew as a holding that does not exist, and it is `<0.00000001` now. Neither row is dropped instead, which would be the same wrong answer with nothing left on screen to question it. Both figures are rendered where every figure here is rendered, so the tables, a venue's own view and what the model is handed moved together and cannot disagree about a balance.
- **The opening screen gave three different answers about one stored credential.** With a Circle key still on disk, the banner read `Connected: circle, hyperliquid, aave`, the status line under it said `2 venues` and `1 failed`, and eight rows above that the same screen said `REMOVED — 1 venue(s) tula no longer reads. Nothing was asked of them, and nothing failed.` Removed, failed and never-asked are three different things, and this is the one screen where they had not been told apart. The banner now names only the venues this build reads, and names a removed one on a line of its own with `/forget <venue>` beside it — the key is still yours and still on disk, and nothing else on an opening screen would mention it until a command goes and reads the store. The status line counts those same venues off that same split, so the two can no longer state different numbers about one store, and it reports what was removed as removed rather than as an outage at a venue nothing was asked of. A venue that is connected and answered with nothing is counted as the venue it is; it had been counted off the rows that came back, so it read as `0 venues`.
- **A Hyperliquid-only reader was never told HyperEVM goes unread.** A holding on HyperEVM, Hyperliquid's own EVM chain, is two linked balances — a HyperCore spot balance and an EVM ERC-20 — and only the HyperCore side is read, so a token bridged across leaves the spot row and nothing replaces it. That gap was declared by the wallet connector, as one of the chains an address is not read on, and the disclosure is built from the venues you connected — so somebody who connected Hyperliquid and no address was shown a spot book with nothing beside it saying half of it might be elsewhere. The Hyperliquid connector declares it now, in its own words, so it is named whether or not a wallet is connected.

### Security

- **A name tula had to clean is now said out loud, with the venue that sent it.** An asset name arrives from outside — a venue's own listing, the configured token list, or an Aave reserve contract answering over whichever node that chain is pointed at — so anything invisible or capable of repainting a line is stripped out of it and what is left is cut to the width of the column. The row then joined the book reading as tula's own word for the asset: a ticker carrying a right-to-left override became a clean three letters and nothing anywhere said so. The model was already told which fields carry text tula did not write; the reader, on the surface that answers with no API key at all, was told nothing. `positions`, `exposure`, `breaks`, `shock`, `refresh`, `venues` and a venue's own `status` now carry an **ALTERED** block naming the venue, what was done — characters removed, cut to fit, or nothing printable left at all — and the cleaned name exactly as the table draws it, so the two match by eye. What the venue actually sent is never printed back: that string is precisely the one that repaints a line, and rendering it to show you it was dangerous is the attack the cap exists to stop. Where the row came off a chain the block names the `TULA_<CHAIN>_RPC` that chooses the node, because whoever answers as one writes every reserve symbol tula reads on that chain, and that variable is settable from a shell profile, a dotfiles repo or a devcontainer. Nothing is missing and nothing failed, so the first line of the block says so and the exit code stays zero — it is not `INCOMPLETE`, not `REMOVED`, and not a coverage gap, and not a venue connected and holding nothing.
- **The check that keeps stand-in data out of a shipped build could not catch the one name the fixture it stands against was spelled with.** `guard.sh` refuses "demo", "dummy", "fake", "toy" and three more anywhere in shipped source, bounded by `\b` — and `_` is a word character, so `TULA_DEMO`, which is exactly how the removed demo fixture was registered and how `tasks/foundations/05-demo-fixture.md` says it would be spelled again, matched nothing at all. The task cites this check as the evidence the fixture stays gone. The bound is letters now, `guard-test.sh` plants `TULA_DEMO` and requires the check to name it, and the tree still passes clean.
- **A Binance key that can take a margin loan and move funds between wallets was accepted as read-only.** `enableMargin` reads like a permission to view margin and is not one: the box it belongs to on Binance's own key page says "Enable Margin Loan, Repay & Transfer". tula parsed the flag and never looked at it, and never looked at `enableInternalTransfer` or `permitsUniversalTransfer` either — so a key that could borrow against the account and push funds to a sub-account passed the read-only gate clean, because none of those three is called trading and none is called withdrawal. All three are refused now, by name, saying which box to untick. `KeyScope` gained the axis they sit on, so the gate has somewhere to put a power that is neither a trade nor a withdrawal.
- **The React DevTools backend is not in the binary.** ink connects to it when `DEV=true`, and `bun build --compile` bundles that import whenever the package resolves at build time — which it did, because ink declares it as an optional peer and `bun install` supplies one whether a project asks for it or not. The released binary carried the backend and `ws://localhost:8097`, so an ambient `DEV=true` — a variable nothing here sets or clears — would have had the process that holds every venue key open a bidirectional inspect-and-mutate channel over its own React tree, to whatever was listening on that port. It is patched out of ink rather than left to the runtime guard, and `scripts/binary-audit.sh` fails the build if a future dependency puts a listener back: CI runs it on the compiled binary, and `release-build.sh` on all four targets before an archive exists.
- **Re-running the install line no longer confirms a tampered build.** The short cut that skips the download asked only whether a version directory and a launcher symlink existed. It did not ask where the launcher pointed, or whether the binary was the one that had been verified — so a tree somebody had swapped a binary into was reported back as "already installed", and the documented way to repair a suspect install was the thing that blessed it. The launcher must now point at that version, and the binary must still hash to what the checksum and attestation passed.
- **A venue's credentials are no longer one keystroke, or one stray click, away from being deleted.** `disconnect` is a row under every connected venue in the `/` menu and a runnable entry in ctrl+k, so arrowing one row past `status` and pressing Enter erased that venue's key from disk with nothing in between — and so did a single left-click anywhere in the menu, where pointer tracking is live for as long as the list is up. An exchange shows a secret key once, when it is created, so that is the same cost as losing it. Every path that takes a credential off disk — `/<venue> disconnect`, `/<source> disconnect` and `/forget <venue>` — now says what is about to go and waits for the name of it to be typed. Not Enter: Enter is the key that arrived there, and pressing it again is what anyone does when a press looks like it missed, so a second Enter keeps the credential and says so. Not a single letter either, which is what a hand resting on a trackpad produces. A venue read from a public address is asked about by that address, which is public and is the whole of what is stored; a keyed venue is told where the key is deleted from and that making a new one is the way back. Nothing is asked where nothing is stored. `/login`'s "Sign out" is the same shape one notch down — one row under whatever the cursor was on, and an `sk-ant-` key is likewise shown once — and now asks with "Keep the key" already under the cursor. The command line is the second way to the same delete and had no gate at all: `tula forget <venue>` and `tula <venue> disconnect` erased a key the exchange that issued it will not show again, so a venue mistyped there, or a script carrying a stale line, was enough. Both now ask for the venue's name on a terminal, and where there is no terminal to ask on they refuse and name what to type instead: `--yes`. A piped stdin is not consent, and reading one as a yes is how an unattended run deletes something nobody named.
- **The connect prompt no longer tells you to pipe an exchange key in.** With no terminal to type on, it printed `printf 'KEY\nSECRET\n' | tula connect <venue>` — a line that puts a key that reads your whole book into your shell history and, while it runs, into the process list, where both outlive the command. `src/index.ts` already refuses a price-source API key on the command line in those words, and the two disagreeing meant the stricter one was decoration. A secret field is now refused without a terminal, with the reason. A public address is not a secret, so the three venues that read one still connect unattended.
- **The installer refuses an install tree other users can write to.** `~/.tula` holds the binary that reads `credentials.json`, and mode 600 on that file is worth nothing if somebody else can replace the process that opens it — which is the argument the credential store already refuses a group-writable config directory on. `mkdir -p` took whatever the umask gave it and nothing looked afterwards.
- **`/update install` refuses the same tree the installer does, and leaves it the same way.** It is the second way onto the same disk and it was the weaker one: it wrote a new version straight into `~/.tula` with no check on who else can write there, and created the directory under whatever umask the shell had — so the tree holding the binary that reads `credentials.json` could be one anybody on the machine can replace a binary in, which is the state the installer refuses outright and the credential store refuses for the config directory. The install, bin and version directories are now checked before anything is downloaded, refused with the same two reasons, and the modes are set rather than inherited. Two paths that disagree about what they will accept means the stricter one is decoration.
- **Installing over a launcher that is a symlink to a directory works.** `ln -sf` follows such a link and writes the new one *inside* the directory, so the launcher went on pointing where it did while the installer reported the new version installed to it.
- **The agent boundary is enforced by following the imports, not by grepping two names.** The promise that the agent layer cannot reach a connector or the credential store was two `grep`s over `src/agent`, which a re-export, an import that reaches the store through a module holding one, a specifier assembled at runtime, or simply renaming the directory each walked past — the last of those leaving both checks disabled and reporting nothing. It is a transitive walk now, and `guard-test.sh` plants all four. The boundary held in the code either way; the check did not.
- **What a venue wrote is marked as such in the data, not only in a rule.** An asset symbol and a venue's error text were already bounded and stripped before they reached the model, and then arrived as ordinary JSON values: the only thing saying they were data rather than instructions was a sentence in the system prompt, which holds for exactly as long as the model obeys it. Every tool result now names the paths those values sit at, in a list beside them — `positions[].asset`, `incomplete.failed_venues[]`. A list of names rather than anything wrapped around the values, for two reasons: the model is required to quote a figure back word for word, so a mark inside the text becomes part of what you read, and a venue is free to spell its token as the mark and close it, which a list of names has nothing to escape out of. The list answers two cases it used to be silent on. A symbol shaped like a figure — a venue may list its token as `$1,234.00` — is named as a name somebody chose, never quotable as a number tula computed. A symbol that is empty is reported as the empty name the venue sent, not as a field tula failed to fill. The reason this is worth doing at all is that it does not depend on the model: a rule in a prompt has to be re-earned from every provider, and a label in the data does not. A field carrying such text and not naming it fails the build: where the text ends up is a data-flow question and no grep reaches it, so the check runs every tool over a book whose every venue-written string carries a sentinel and fails on any path the result does not declare. `scripts/guard-test.sh` plants exactly that field and requires the check to name it, the way it already plants an order endpoint and requires `guard.sh` to refuse it.
- **The cap on an asset symbol counted the wrong thing, twice.** A venue spells its own tickers, and the 32-character bound was 32 UTF-16 code units — so a symbol built from astral code points was cut between the halves of a surrogate pair, and a lone surrogate reached the screen *and* the model, out of the one string that exists to be bounded. The same bound passed 32 CJK characters, which are 64 cells wide and push every column right of them off the row. A cut now falls between whole grapheme clusters and stops at 32 of them or at 32 cells, whichever comes first.
- **`INCOMPLETE` is no longer the part of an answer that gets held back.** An entry longer than twelve rows keeps the first twelve and collapses the rest to a count, and the block naming a venue that failed is appended last — so eleven positions and one failed read drew a table that looked like the whole book, `… 6 more lines`, and nothing at all about the venue that was never read. The net notional went with it. `/breaks` was worse: a ranked liquidation table reading as complete while the venue most likely to liquidate you had never been read. The caveat now sits under the count rather than behind it, whatever the length of the answer above it, and a command that states its failures inside its own table instead is not truncated at all.
- **The installer's own header says what it actually does.** It claimed every step that could hand you a different binary is checked and that it refuses rather than warns. The checksum is always checked and always refuses; provenance needs the GitHub CLI, present and signed in, and without it the install goes ahead and says so. That claim had been retracted everywhere else and survived here by two words.

Positions from the markets beside Core carry their own label — `aave-prime` next to `aave` — so `/aave positions` still shows all of them and `breaks` can say which market to act on, and each market's health factor now belongs to that market's collateral rather than being shared. A debt secures only the collateral in the market it was borrowed in, which is the relationship that actually exists. A market whose read fails now fails the venue rather than passing for one holding nothing.
- **The USDC behind a Hyperliquid perp is in your book.** The connector read `withdrawable`, which is what may be taken out *right now*, so every dollar posted against an open perp vanished from the portfolio — on a leveraged account, most of it. It reads the account's raw USDC balance instead, which includes what is behind a position. Not the account value: that carries unrealized profit the perp legs already hold in their own exposure, and counting it here would report one price move twice.
- **The install page had the Homebrew safety rule backwards.** It said a build found to be wrong is skipped by *not* promoting the next one; the release workflow does the opposite, and skips it *by* promoting the next one. A reader following the page would have left themselves on the bad build. The same page said there were two names to install from when there are three, and the security page's list of what the binary talks to did not mention the one download it makes on request — `/update install` — or the variable that stops the daily check.
- **Asking the model about one venue no longer hides that venue's sub-accounts.** Every command has always matched `kraken-margin` under `kraken`; the agent's own position lookup compared the two for equality instead, so a question about one venue silently dropped its sub-account rows and answered as though that were the whole book.

### Security

- **A venue's text cannot smuggle in what a control-character filter never looked at.** Symbols and venue error text were stripped of the control and format categories, which covers bidi overrides and zero-width characters but leaves three things that are neither: the 256 variation selectors, which encode a byte apiece and have been used to hide payloads in package metadata; the line and paragraph separators, which forge a new line in a view built out of lines, so one venue's failure can pose as the message above it; and the invisible fillers that render as nothing at all. All of them go now, text is composed first so two spellings of one symbol reach the book as one asset rather than two, and a printing symbol a real venue ships — Arbitrum's USDT is spelled `USD₮0` — is left exactly as it is.

### Security

- **A venue's key can no longer be carried off by a redirect.** `fetch` strips `Authorization` when a redirect crosses to another host and strips nothing else, so Binance's `X-MBX-APIKEY`, Kraken's `API-Key` and CoinMarketCap's `X-CMC_PRO_API_KEY` would all have been re-sent to whatever the `Location` named. Anything able to shape a venue's response — an intercepting proxy, a rogue CA, an open redirect at the venue's edge — could have collected the key by answering `302`. Redirects are now refused outright; the two requests that legitimately follow one, both in `/update` and neither carrying a credential, opt back in and `guard.sh` fails the build on a third.
- **The model endpoint is pinned.** The Anthropic SDK reads `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` from the environment, so one line in a shell profile — from a dotfiles repo, a devcontainer, a package's install script — would have sent every question, the whole computed book with it, and the API key to a fourth party, while the screen still said Anthropic. tula now states the host.
- **A venue's error text can no longer repaint the screen that refuses its key.** Error strings from a venue were capped and flattened everywhere except the connect path, which rendered them raw — and Ink draws text verbatim. An escape sequence in a JSON-RPC error from a hostile `TULA_ETH_RPC` could rewrite the line above it, which at that moment is the scope refusal. Both connect paths now go through the same cap as the rest.
- **A published checksum is checked for being a checksum before it is printed.** It is bytes from a downloaded file, splitting on whitespace does not split on ESC, and the message carrying it is drawn in the one case the checksum exists for — so a failing archive could have repainted the line reporting the failure.
- **An address is checked against its own checksum.** A single mistyped character used to be accepted and read somebody else's book under it, silently and with every number beside it correct. EIP-55 is verified where a mixed-case address carries one; an address typed in one case has no checksum to check and is still accepted, since refusing those only teaches people to lower-case until it is taken.
- **An attestation now has to come from the release workflow, not merely from this repository.** Any workflow holding `attestations: write` could mint one the installer accepted, so a proposed change to CI was enough to sign an arbitrary archive. `install.sh` pins the signing workflow.

- **A venue's endpoint URL no longer travels with its error.** `TULA_ETH_RPC` is offered as the way to point tula at your own Ethereum node, and the two providers people actually point it at — Alchemy and Infura — carry the API key in the URL path. A non-200 from that node was reported as `RPC <the whole URL> returned HTTP 429`, which is printed on screen and handed to the model as a failed-venue string. Every such message now names the host and nothing else, which is what `core/http.ts` already did for a timeout. The token list is the same fix.
- **A credential is no longer written into a directory anyone can replace it in.** The store refuses to *read* out of a world-writable config directory, but the check sat after the branch that returns early when there is no file yet — so the very first `connect` on a machine where `~/.config/tula` already existed loose wrote the key into it and reported success, and the refusal arrived on the next read, with the secret already on disk. It refuses before the first write now.
- **An attestation now only ever exists for a published release, so one that verifies is one that shipped.** A rehearsal run of the release workflow used to mint one too, and `install.sh` accepts any archive carrying an attestation for this repository — so a build made from an arbitrary branch, and never released, would have verified as genuine.
- **`TULA_VERSION` cannot redirect the download.** curl resolves dot-segments in a path, so a version containing `../` walked the download base out of this repository and onto another one — `checksums.txt` included, which made the check agree with whatever it had just fetched. The origin being hardcoded is the argument the installer rests on; the version is now checked before it reaches a URL, as `/update` already checks the tag it reads.

### Fixed

- **tula hands the terminal back, however it ends.** Pointing at a list turns on mouse reporting, and the undo was a React cleanup — which covers unmounting and nothing else. Closing the window (SIGHUP), a `kill` (SIGTERM) or a crash left the mode on afterwards, so moving the pointer wrote escape codes at the user's own shell prompt and selecting text stopped working, with no way to know that `printf '\e[?1006l\e[?1003l'` was the cure. The undo is now registered with the process as well, and re-raises so the exit code is still the one the signal meant. Bun does not reach `exit` from an uncaught throw the way Node does, so crashes are covered separately.
- **A swept mouse no longer types into the command line.** Reports were matched one per chunk, but mode 1003 reports every movement and a hand crossing the screen sends them faster than stdin is drained — so a run of them failed to parse and went in as the punctuation it looks like, `[<35;10;5M[<35;11;5M…`. They are read as a stream now, and a report split across two chunks is held rather than typed.
- **Every failed venue keeps its own way out.** The suggestion to run `/<venue> status` was suppressed when *any* failure already named a remedy, and a venue quoting a URL counts as one — so a single failure mentioning a link took the way out away from every other venue that had failed beside it.
- **A venue that failed is no longer described as an empty account.** It was counted among the ones that "returned nothing", so a book nobody could open was reported as "connected and returned nothing — either the account is empty, or it is not the one you trade with", two lines above an `INCOMPLETE` block saying it had never been reached.
- **A bug in tula says it is one.** An unexpected error reached the screen as a bare line — `TypeError: x is not a function` — with nothing to do about it and nowhere to send it. It now says it is a bug and names the issue tracker.
- **Every error names a way out.** A dead Ethereum node, a Hyperliquid 5xx, a reserve list that came back empty and an incomplete stored credential all stated a problem and stopped. Coinbase's two signature-format checks went the other way: they are tula disagreeing with itself, nothing a user can act on, so they keep their stack trace instead of posing as advice.
- **Hyperliquid's clock cannot make a stale snapshot read as fresh.** Its rows are stamped with the venue's own time, and a venue running ahead of the machine pinned every one at "0s ago" while the data behind it aged.

- **`/` and ctrl+k now list the same commands.** They were built from two functions and disagreed about which commands existed: after connecting a venue, ctrl+k offered `/hyperliquid connect` — the row that replaces a stored address — while typing `/h` offered `/hyperliquid` and stopped. The subcommands were reachable, behind a space nothing on screen mentioned. Both lists are now the one surface; only the matching differs, prefix under the input line and fuzzy in the dialog.
- **Connecting says what it is doing.** Storing the credential is instant; reading the venue is not, and that read ran with the spinner off — so the screen sat on "Connected Hyperliquid." with an idle prompt for as long as the venue took, then printed a book. The wait is now covered end to end and names the venue being read.

- **A position already being liquidated no longer reads as the safest row on screen.** Below a health factor of 1 the move to liquidation is zero, and an unsigned zero renders `+0.0%` — in a column where a plus means the price must *rise* to reach the trigger. An Aave account at 0.50, underwater and being liquidated, was drawn beside positions with room to spare and handed to the model as a 0% rise. Such a row now says `liquidatable now`, in the tables and in the tool result both.
- **A zero or unparseable price is no longer a price.** `new Decimal(0)` is an object and therefore truthy, so a zero passed every downstream guard and rendered a real holding as `$0.00` with no unpriced note — the defect this project names first. `NaN` and `Infinity` do not throw either, and one of them turned every total in the book into `$NaN`. All four price sources now go through one gate, which also fixes a `null` quote from CryptoCompare throwing a raw `DecimalError` that cost every other price in the batch.
- **`/refresh` says when it did not refresh everything.** It reported a count and exited 0 while venues were failing — the one command somebody runs *to recover from* a failure was the one hiding it. It now carries the same `INCOMPLETE` block every other command does. Its venue count was wrong in both directions as well, counting sub-account labels twice and connected-but-empty venues not at all.
- **The model is told when the book it is quoting is incomplete.** Every command has carried `INCOMPLETE` since the first release; the agent's numeric tools carried nothing, so a book missing a venue was narrated as a total with a system-prompt rule as the only thing standing against it. All four now return what failed alongside the figures.
- **An Aave reserve that failed to load is no longer read as a zero balance.** A failed `eth_call` inside a batch returns null, and null defaulted to a zero quantity, eighteen decimals and the symbol `UNKNOWN` — so a dropped *debt* leg made the book look richer and safer while the health factor beside it still rendered, two `UNKNOWN` reserves netted together as one asset, and a six-decimal balance scaled by ten-to-the-eighteen read as nothing. The venue is now reported failed instead.
- **A Coinbase account holding more than 250 currencies is read to the end.** The first page was taken as the whole account, so the tail vanished into a net exposure that reported itself complete.
- **USDD is priced rather than assumed.** It sat beside USD in the set of tickers worth one dollar by definition, under a comment that justifies USD alone. It is a priced, de-peggable asset, and pinning it valued it at par through exactly the event its holder needs the real number for.
- **The site stopped offering an address-only venue a key, again.** The fix shipped in the binary and the homepage kept the old wording, because the fixture behind the test that pins that page built its venue rows without the flag that separates an address from a key — so the test compared the page against a drawing of the wrong build. `/<venue> status` also went on claiming a key had been "checked as read-only" for Kraken, Stripe and Circle, which expose no way to check exactly that, contradicting what the connect screen had already said honestly.

- **A slow `/update install` no longer ends in a stack trace.** The deadline `request()` carries is a venue-poll number, and the abort signal it attaches stays on the response body — so any connection that could not pull tens of megabytes inside 15 seconds threw a raw `TimeoutError` past every handler that knows what a tula failure looks like. Downloads now carry a deadline of their own, and a download that stops part-way says so and installs nothing. An unpack that fails says the same rather than printing a stack trace.
- **A Binance call that fails is no longer read as an empty result.** The account loads from two calls, and the second was wrapped in a catch meant for a key without futures permission — which also swallowed a timeout, a 5xx and a gateway error page, loading the account with no `INCOMPLETE` and exit 0. Only Binance's own permission codes are treated that way now. (Binance's futures permission grants futures *trading*, so a key that could read those positions is refused at connect; the path is kept correct against the day that changes.)
- **A Homebrew formula is never rendered with an empty checksum.** `set -e` does not fire for a command substitution inside a heredoc, so a `checksums.txt` missing a platform produced a valid-looking formula carrying `sha256 ""` and exited 0 — which the release job commits and pushes, and every `brew install` on that platform then fails as a checksum mismatch, after the release is already public.
- **A venue holding nothing no longer reports an age for data it does not have.** `/` and ctrl+k drew `0 balances · 0s ago`, because reducing over no rows answers with the current time.
- **`breaks` and `shock` no longer answer for a book they never read.** With nothing connected, `breaks` said "Nothing here can be liquidated" and `shock` priced a $0.00 portfolio and reported that nothing liquidated at that level — three true figures adding up to reassurance about a portfolio that was never fetched. Both now say what `positions` and `exposure` already said, and name the way out.
- **A venue that failed is no longer reported as safe.** `/<venue> breaks` and `/<venue> positions` left out the `INCOMPLETE` block every other command carries and exited 0, so a lending venue nobody could reach answered "Nothing at aave can be liquidated" — which to a script is the definition of fine.
- **Address-only venues are no longer offered a key.** Wallet, Hyperliquid and Aave are read from a public address and hold no credential, but the menu and ctrl+k described connecting them as "add or replace this venue's read-only key", and `/<venue> status` claimed a key scope had been verified. A tool that asks a wallet for a key is the shape of a phishing page. `status` also reported "oldest, 0s ago" for a venue holding nothing, because the age of no rows was read as the current time.
- **`/forget <venue>` takes effect in the shell it was typed in.** Only `/<venue> disconnect` refreshed what the session believed was connected, so after `/forget` the menu went on listing the venue as live and `status` went on answering from the connected branch.
- **Installing twice leaves one line in your shell profile.** The check for a line already added looked for the default spelling of the path rather than the one it had written, so an install under `TULA_INSTALL_DIR` appended another block every run.
- **The installer names every tool it needs.** A missing `grep` surfaced as the checksum lookup coming back empty, which the script reports as an archive missing from `checksums.txt` — a missing tool read to the user as a supply-chain attack.
- **One wording for the trading promise, and it is the one that can be extended.** `install.sh` closed with "it cannot place an order or move funds" — the flat form every other surface avoids, because it becomes a withdrawn security promise the day trading ships — and nothing was checking that file. `/about` had drifted to a fourth phrasing of its own. Every surface now carries "placing trades will come later", and the test that holds them together accepts only that.
- **npm is described as the channel it is.** The install page said npm proved nothing about who built the binary and sent the reader to another channel for that, while the release publishes it with `npm publish --provenance` — sigstore provenance the reader could have checked. What is true is narrower: the GitHub attestation covers the release archive, and npm repacks it into something that attestation cannot describe.
- **The verify command carries the flags the page argues for.** The install page spends a legend on `--proto '=https'` and `--tlsv1.2`, then handed the reader a bare `curl -fLO` for the one download they make by hand — the download whose whole purpose is checking who built the binary. `install.sh` printed the same bare command in its unverified fallback.
- **The README's security claims match the code again.** Its egress list was exhaustive and named neither the Ethereum node, the token list nor the daily release check; its attestation promise said the installer "refuses rather than warns", which is what the security page had already been corrected for and what happens only where the GitHub CLI is present and signed in.
- **Every asset symbol is bounded.** Binance and Hyperliquid passed the venue's raw string straight into a position, and a symbol is printed in every table and sent to the model with every position. Every connector now arrives at one place that caps it at 32 characters and strips control characters, rather than one chance per connector to forget.
- **A failed venue's own error text is bounded too.** It is drawn on screen and returned to the model by `get_venue_status`, and its content is whatever the venue put in an error body — Binance's `msg`, 120 characters of a Coinbase response, Kraken's error array. Now capped at 200 characters and flattened to a single line in `src/cli/session.ts`, so a venue cannot answer with something that reads as a second message. It also fixes the rendering: `/status` prints one indented line per failure, and a multi-line message used to break out of that list.
- **A token symbol read from a contract is bounded before anything renders or reads it.** `decodeString` took the length prefix from the response and returned whatever came back, and an Aave reserve's symbol becomes a position's asset name — rendered in the tables and handed to the model as a tool result. Whoever answers as the Ethereum RPC chose both the prefix and the bytes. It is now capped at 32 bytes with control characters and line breaks stripped, so a symbol cannot arrive carrying its own line. The security page had claimed memos, NFT metadata and protocol descriptions were the hostile surface; tula reads none of those, and did not mention the one string it does read. Both say the same true thing now.
- **`SECURITY.md` no longer promises a 72-hour acknowledgement.** One person maintains this; a policy that misses its own stated clock is worse than one that never set it. It now says what it can keep — a reply usually within a week, and what to do if a week passes — and names the third injection surface it had left out.
- **`SECURITY.md` names every host tula contacts.** It said two destinations, both of which you choose, and that nothing else was contacted to build the view — omitting the public Ethereum RPC and the token list, which are defaults rather than choices and each see the address being read. `guard.sh` also now fails when the `gh attestation verify` example in `SECURITY.md`, `README.md` or the install page names a release other than this one: a reader copies that line verbatim, and a stale filename reports as a failed verification.
- **The `gh attestation verify` command the installer prints now names something that is actually attested.** The attestation's subject is the release archive; the installer pointed the command at the extracted binary, so anyone following it to check provenance for themselves got a rejection and had no way to tell that from a real one. It now prints the download and the archive. The security page said every channel could be checked that way, which is not true of npm — that repackages the binary into its own tarball, which carries no attestation; the page and `SECURITY.md` say so and point at the install script or Homebrew where provenance has to be proven.
- **The credential store no longer follows a link, or trusts a directory anyone can write to.** `stat` reports a symlink's *target*, so a link planted at `credentials.json` passed the 600-mode check and then took the next write wherever it pointed — demonstrated writing a real key and secret into an attacker-chosen file, silently. It now `lstat`s and refuses anything but a regular file, refuses a config directory that is group- or world-writable, checks both before reading rather than after, and writes through a temporary file and a rename so an interrupted write cannot truncate the store.
- **An unreachable venue hung the whole tool.** No outbound call had a deadline, so a venue that accepted the connection and went quiet — a rate-limited exchange, a saturated public RPC, a half-open NAT connection — blocked the refresh for as long as the OS was willing to wait. Measured at 76s for one unreachable RPC, with the shell spinning and no way out but Ctrl-C. Every call now goes through one `request()` with a 15s deadline, and `guard.sh` fails the build on a bare `fetch`. The deadline is a raced timer rather than `AbortSignal` alone, because the signal bounds the wait for a response and not for a connection: against a black-holed address a 3s signal took 75s to fire. A one-shot command now also exits once its output is written, instead of waiting on the socket of a venue that already failed.
- **An Anthropic outage printed its raw JSON envelope.** `Error: {"type":"error","error":{"type":"overloaded_error",...}}` tells someone with money at risk nothing they can act on. Failures are now translated — which side failed, whether waiting helps, and that commands answer without the model — and a transient overload is retried five times before anyone sees it. A failed request no longer leaves the abandoned question in history, where the next one would be answered in its place.
- **Plain English could answer "nothing is connected" about a connected venue**, because nothing forced a first load before a question. Relatedly, `/exposure` reported an empty book straight after a venue connected: storing credentials re-armed the open-with-state effect, which ran against the pre-connect cache and printed "nothing to measure" over a wallet that had just returned four positions.
- **An empty book told you to connect a venue you had already connected.** The message now depends on the credential store: no venue is a connect prompt; a connected venue holding nothing names the venue, its status command and `/refresh`.
- **A connected venue holding nothing vanished from `/venues`.** Rows were built from positions, so a venue that succeeded and returned zero was indistinguishable from one never connected — the silent gap this tool exists to close.
- **Every command from the menu took two Enters.** With the `/` menu open, Enter completed the highlighted command the way tab does — the menu closed, the line filled in, and nothing ran until it was pressed again, which reads as the first press having been missed. Enter now runs what is highlighted and tab is what completes it, the split ctrl+k already used. A command with arguments left to supply is still only completed: they cannot be guessed.
- **ctrl+k answers the mouse.** It was keyboard-only: nothing asked the terminal for mouse reports, so a scroll went to the terminal's own scrollback — which the dialog is covering — and the list sat still. The pointer now reaches it the way it reaches any other dialog. The row under it lights up, a click runs that row, a click on the screen outside closes it, and the wheel scrolls one row a notch against a scrollbar that says how far down the list this is. Tracking is on while the palette is open and only then: it takes drag-to-select and scrollback away from the terminal for as long as it is on, and the transcript is the part people copy numbers out of. A report arriving with the palette closed is swallowed rather than typed onto the line.
- **So does the `/` menu.** It sits in the frame rather than floating over it, so where it lands depends on where the transcript left the cursor — at the bottom of the screen once the session has filled it, part-way down before that, and a click that guessed would run a different command than the one under the pointer. The terminal is asked where its cursor is, which is the one fixed point tying the pointer's absolute rows to a block drawn wherever the frame ended up. A terminal that does not answer leaves the menu on the keyboard and the wheel, which need no such anchor.
- **The wheel moves the list, not the cursor.** The window used to be derived from the selection, so the first notches walked the cursor down rows already on screen and nothing moved — a list that ignores the wheel until it abruptly does not. Scrolling moves the window itself and carries the selection only as far as keeping it on screen requires: enter still runs something you can see. The arrow keys get the other half of that fix — the cursor moves inside the window and takes it along at the edges, rather than the list sliding on every press.
- **ctrl+k listed two "price source" sections, and offered CoinGecko a key it has none of.** The palette put every subcommand in a run after every top-level command, so the sections it draws headings for were reached twice — once for the sources, again for everything under them. Each source now carries its own subcommands, and a keyless source is no longer offered `connect` or `disconnect`: there is nothing to paste and nothing to forget, and `/coingecko disconnect` had read "fall back to CoinGecko". The "N more below" count also read off the whole list rather than the visible window, so it never moved as you arrowed down and still promised more at the last row — which reads as a list that does not scroll.
- **A typed space was deleted before it reached the input line.** The paste handler normalised newlines then trimmed, so a lone `" "` became `""`, making `/shock ETH -20` and every venue subcommand impossible to type.
- **An `ant auth login` profile is now recognised as a credential.** The SDK reads it on its own, so a signed-in user was told they had none and asked for an API key.
- Venue subcommands did not work one-shot: `tula wallet status` printed the usage banner, and once the parser knew the venues, every one still reported "not connected" because the dispatcher was never handed the stored ones.
- A misused command exits non-zero — `shock ETH banana` reported success, so a typo in a script looked like it worked.

### Removed

- **The demo venue and every piece of fake data.** Any public address exercises the full cross-domain path against real data, so a fixture sitting beside real venues was a liability rather than a convenience. `TULA_DEMO` is gone; the cross-domain scenario survives as a connector defined inside the test file that asserts on it.

### Notes

- `WETH` normalizes to `ETH` — redeemable one-for-one and trustlessly, so it is the same exposure. `wstETH` and `weETH` are not, and are left alone; `WBTC` carries custodian risk and is priced separately.
- Stripe quotes in each currency's minor unit and not all have two, so dividing everything by 100 would report a JPY balance as a hundredth of itself.
- bun's `node:crypto` rejects `dsaEncoding: 'ieee-p1363'`, so Coinbase's ECDSA signatures are converted from DER to JOSE by hand; without it every request would present as a rejected key.
- Address-only venues report `canTrade: false` and `canWithdraw: false` as proven facts — there is no credential to over-scope. That is a real contrast with Kraken, and the connect screen says which kind you are looking at.
- Without an Anthropic key the product is unchanged except that plain English is unavailable. Every view has a command behind it.
- The agent surface was planned for the execution milestone and pulled forward to the shell. The hard boundary did not move with it.
- The compiled binary carries `react-devtools-core` (+2.3 MB): Ink imports it statically from a module it only loads when `DEV=true`, and marking it external breaks the binary at startup.
- The risk-engine work landed alongside breadth rather than after it. Getting the maths right against a fixture was cheaper than discovering it wrong against a live perp position.
- `KeyScope` is tri-state. Kraken exposes no endpoint reporting a key's permissions, and every endpoint gated on trade permission mutates an order, so `canTrade` is `unknown` rather than guessed at. Withdraw scope is provable, and is proven.
- Kraken margin and open orders are not read yet, so on a margin account this is not a complete Kraken picture.
